# AAC Support page: accuracy review (August 2026)

Reviewed against ASHA guidance and primary research. Claim-by-claim verdicts; page updated same day.

## Verified and kept (now cited more precisely)
1. **"AAC will not stop my child from talking."** Millar, Light & Schlosser (2006), JSLHR 49(2):248–264. Meta-analysis, 23 studies / 67 individuals; best-evidence subset 6 studies / 27 cases: **89% gained speech, 11% no change, 0% decreased**. Authors note gains were "modest" with possible ceiling effects. Page now states the exact figures AND the modest-gains caveat. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16671842/
2. **Autism-specific version.** Schlosser & Wendt (2008), AJSLP 17(3): majority of studies showed speech increases, none reported a decline. Kept.
3. **No prerequisites.** ASHA/NJC states there are "no specific prerequisites (e.g., age, cognitive, linguistic, motor)" for starting AAC; ASHA early-intervention guidance repeats it for birth-to-three. Kept; NJC page added to sources. https://www.asha.org/njc/aac/
4. **Early introduction.** Romski et al. (2010), JSLHR 53(2):350–364. RCT, 68 toddlers randomized (62 completed), all with <10 spoken words: augmented (SGD) groups did better; authors: augmented communication "does not hinder, and actually aids, speech production." Page wording changed from "second year of life" to "toddler years" to match the trial population (24–36 mo). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20360461/
5. **Modeling works.** O'Neill, Light & Pope (2018), JSLHR 61(7):1743–1765. Meta-analysis (26 single-case designs / 88 participants + 2 group designs / 103): aided AAC input "highly effective" across ages and disabilities; mean Tau-U 0.83. Added to sources.
6. **SGD in blended intervention.** Kasari et al. (2014), JAACAP 53(6):635–646: 61 minimally verbal autistic children (5–8 y); adding an SGD produced more spontaneous utterances and novel words. Added to sources.
7. **Communication Bill of Rights** (NJC, hosted by ASHA): kept.
8. **Feature matching, trials, multimodal goal, funding documentation**: consistent with ASHA Practice Portal (AAC). Kept.

## Fixed
1. **"80% of what we say comes from core words"**: vague, and mismatched with the source cited next to it. Banajee, DiCarlo & Stricklin (2003), AAC 19(2):67–73 actually found: 50 toddlers (24–36 mo), **23 words = 96.3% of sampled speech**. Page now uses the real figure, framed as toddler speech.
2. **"5x, model more than you expect"**: no research basis found; invented-sounding. Removed. Replaced with the widely cited Korsten (2011, QIAT Listserv) exposure math: a typical child has ~**4,380 waking hours** of spoken-language exposure by 18 months; an AAC learner modeled to only 2×20–30 min/week would need ~**84 years** to match it. Corroborating hosts: AssistiveWare, Indiana IRCA. https://www.assistiveware.com/learn-aac/roadblock-give-up-too-soon
3. **Device cost.** AzTAP (2026 newsletter): AAC devices "often retail for $4,000 or higher." FAQ updated from vague "several thousand dollars"; funding routes named (AHCCCS/private insurance, DDD, schools, foundations, AzLAT low-interest loans).

## Honest limits (kept visible on page)
- Millar 2006 speech gains were modest; page says so: AAC is a voice, not a speech cure.
- Evidence base is mostly single-case experimental designs plus a few RCTs (Romski 2010; Kasari 2014); direction is consistent across all of them.
- Banajee's 96% is toddler speech specifically; page frames it that way.
