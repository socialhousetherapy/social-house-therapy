# Evaluations page: accuracy review (August 2026)

Same method as aac-review.md: claim-by-claim verdicts against ASHA guidance, federal regulations, and primary research. Page updated same day. House style rule applied and recorded in CLAUDE.md: no em dashes ever; nine were removed from this page and reworded with commas, colons, parentheses, or split sentences. (This file replaces the earlier code/transcript dump, which was stale and fully regenerable from source.)

## Verified and kept (now cited more precisely)
1. **"No single test or score can determine the recommendation."** Backed twice over. IDEA regs require agencies to "use a variety of assessment tools and strategies" and to "not use any single measure or assessment as the sole criterion" for determining disability or program (34 CFR 300.304(b)(1)-(2)). ASHA's Assessment Tools page: clinicians select methods per age, cultural background, language profile, and severity, and compare findings from multiple sources. Both added to the page's Clinical References.
2. **Comprehensive components list** (history and records, interviews, observation, formal and informal measures, communication samples, dynamic assessment, oral mechanism exam, hearing review, functional impact, differential diagnosis, referrals): matches ASHA Preferred Practice Patterns (2004) and the Practice Portal assessment sections. Kept.
3. **Dynamic assessment** ("what helps them learn it"): matches ASHA's description of DA as a test-teach-retest method that helps distinguish difference from disorder (Kapantzoglou et al., 2012, as cited by ASHA). Kept.
4. **"Speaking more than one language does not cause a language disorder."** Kohnert (ASHA Leader, 2006): development in two languages "does not cause language impairment"; a true disorder shows up in all of a child's languages, and bilingual performance must be judged against a bilingual reference. The page's multilingual practices (assess the languages the child uses, interpreter support, weight to descriptive methods when norms don't fit, documenting score limitations) match ASHA's Multilingual Service Delivery portal and IDEA 300.304(c)(1)(ii) (assessment in the child's language unless clearly not feasible). Kept; the ASHA portal URL was updated to its current address.
5. **CAS assessed by a trained SLP; provisional impression appropriate for very young children.** Matches ASHA's CAS Practice Portal and the 2007 technical report (diagnosis by an SLP with motor speech expertise; the three 2007 features are consensus, not validated markers). Kept.
6. **AAC: no prerequisite skills.** Re-verified in aac-review.md (ASHA/NJC). Kept.
7. **Feeding safety note: a clinical evaluation cannot rule out aspiration.** Strongly supported. Calvo et al. (2016), Dev Med Child Neurol systematic review: silent aspiration "may not be recognized during clinical assessment"; no universally accepted standard clinical feeding evaluation, and many are neither validated nor reliable. Printza et al. (2022): clinical evaluation "cannot validly predict aspiration, which is mostly silent." VFSS and FEES are the accepted instrumental methods. Kept, and strengthened (see Fixed #3).
8. **School/IDEA claims.** 34 CFR 300.502(c)(1): a privately obtained evaluation shared with the agency "must be considered by the public agency, if it meets agency criteria, in any decision made with respect to the provision of FAPE." Page matches, including the meets-agency-criteria qualifier most sites omit. Consideration-is-not-adoption matches OSEP policy interpretation. Inline CFR citation added to the aside.
9. **Hearing.** Hearing status affects speech-language development; review hearing history and refer to audiology when information is missing or concerning: consistent with ASHA's Childhood Hearing Screening portal. Kept.

## Fixed
1. **ARFID: "is not diagnosed or treated by an SLP"** was half right. Diagnosis: correct, ARFID is a DSM-5 mental health diagnosis and not an SLP call. Treatment: overstated. Reviews and clinical references consistently place SLPs ON the ARFID interdisciplinary treatment team (swallowing safety, oral-motor, feeding-skill work): Thomas et al. 2023 review; StatPearls 2024; Cleveland Clinic. Reworded: diagnosing ARFID is outside SLP scope; care is interdisciplinary; we may assess and treat co-occurring feeding-skill or swallowing difficulties as part of that team.
2. **CAS: "No single feature confirms childhood apraxia of speech"** understated the evidence. ASHA (citing Murray et al., 2021): "there are no validated diagnostic features that differentiate CAS from other childhood speech sound disorders." Page now says research has not validated any single feature, or list of features, that reliably separates CAS from other speech sound disorders.
3. **Feeding note strengthened**: added that aspiration in young children is frequently silent (no cough or outward sign), which is the specific reason a clinical evaluation cannot rule it out (Calvo 2016; Printza 2022).
4. **References section expanded**: added IDEA 300.304 (the multiple-measures requirement behind the page's central claim), ASHA's Assessment Tools page, and Calvo et al. 2016. Updated the multilingual portal URL. Intro sentence now names peer-reviewed research alongside ASHA and federal regs.
5. **Em dashes removed** in nine places (question card 04, hearing accordion, differential diagnosis accordion, "Questions we differentiate" heading, feeding safety note, school-sharing aside, two FAQ answers, references list).

## Honest limits (kept visible or noted here)
- Evidence on clinical feeding evaluations is not unanimous: one 2024 study (Dahiya et al., low-resource setting) reports clinical feeding assessment can screen for aspiration with 80-100% sensitivity and 68-82% specificity, while the systematic-review evidence (Calvo 2016) finds accuracy unestablished and silent aspiration clinically undetectable. The page keeps the conservative framing: clinical evaluation helps determine next steps but cannot rule out aspiration.
- "Approximately one to two hours" for direct assessment is a description of scheduling practice, not a research standard; the page already says there is no single standard length. Left alone.
- The three CAS consensus features (ASHA 2007) have researcher consensus but are not validated markers; the page deliberately presents no diagnostic checklist.
- The four-questions framing and the 3/8+/1/0 stats are the practice's own organizing language, not clinical claims; no citation needed.
