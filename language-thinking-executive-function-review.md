## New claims verified and kept
1. **Cognitive aspects of communication are within SLP scope.** ASHA Scope of Practice in Speech-Language Pathology (2016) lists cognition among service delivery areas; ASHA 2020 certification standards use the exact list quoted on the page ("attention, memory, sequencing, problem solving, and executive functioning"). Kept.
2. **Three core executive functions: inhibitory control, working memory, cognitive flexibility.** Diamond (2013), Annual Review of Psychology 64:135-168. Kept.
3. **Language and EF are strongly related at every age; neither reliably drives growth in the other; children with language impairment show persistent EF weaknesses.** Gooch, Thompson, Nash, Snowling & Hulme (2016), JCPP 57(2):180-187: 243 children followed ages 4 to 7 (behaviour ratings to age 9), strong concurrent association, high stability, cross-lagged paths small and nonsignificant, EF (not language) predicted later attention/behaviour ratings. Page reports this accurately, including the null cross-lagged finding, which is why the page never claims language therapy improves EF or the reverse. Kept.
4. **EF deficits in DLD: moderate for inhibition, small for flexibility.** Pauls & Archibald (2016), JSLHR 59(5):1074-1086: 46 studies, ages 4 to 14, g = -0.56 inhibition, g = -0.27 flexibility, no moderation by age, severity, or verbal task load. Page says "moderate margin" and "smaller one." Supported also by Henry, Messer & Nash (2012), JCPP 53(1):37-45. Kept.
5. **Children with ADHD score about one standard deviation below peers on language measures.** Korrel, Mueller, Silk, Anderson & Sciberras (2017), JCPP 58(6):640-654: 21 studies, 1,209 children with ADHD; weighted mean effect sizes 1.04 overall, 1.23 expressive, 0.97 receptive, 0.98 pragmatic. Page says "around one standard deviation for expressive, receptive, and pragmatic language alike." Kept.
6. **Task-based and rating-based EF measures barely agree.** Toplak, West & Stanovich (2013), JCPP 54(2):131-143: 20 studies, 286 correlations, 24% significant, median r = .19. Page states "about .19" and the authors' interpretation (efficiency under ideal conditions vs typical performance in real life). Kept.
7. **Working-memory training does not transfer to language, reading, or arithmetic.** Melby-Lervåg, Redick & Hulme (2016), Perspectives on Psychological Science 11(4):512-534: 87 publications, 145 comparisons; reliable near/intermediate transfer only; no convincing far transfer against treated controls. Drives the "no brain-training apps" stance. Kept.
8. **SLPs do not diagnose ADHD.** Scope statement, consistent with the evaluations page. Who does: AAP clinical practice guideline (Wolraich et al., 2019), Pediatrics 144(4):e20192528, diagnosis by the clinician using DSM-5 criteria with information from parents, teachers, and other settings. Kept.

## Deliberately not claimed
- That language therapy improves EF, or that EF work improves language (Gooch 2016 found no cross-lagged effects). The page says the two are treated together because they co-occur and share the same tasks.
- That the language-vs-attention pairs are diagnostic. Framed as questions we ask, with an explicit caveat that many children belong in both columns.
- Any ADHD prevalence figure.
- Any EF test or rating scale by name.
- Any outcome promise for EF strategy work.
