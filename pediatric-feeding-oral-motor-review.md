# Pediatric Feeding & Oral-Motor page: accuracy review (August 2026)

Reviewed against the ASHA Practice Portal (Pediatric Feeding and Swallowing), the PFD consensus, and primary research. Claim-by-claim verdicts; page updated same day. Same process as the other page reviews.

## Verified and kept
1. **Four domains of feeding (medical, nutrition, feeding skill, psychosocial).** Goday et al. (2019) PFD consensus definition, JPGN 68(1):124-129, cited. Exactly the four consensus domains. Kept.
2. **Picky eater vs problem feeder distinctions (~30+ foods vs ~20 or fewer, dropped foods not regained, whole textures refused, distress at new foods).** Standard SOS-approach clinical criteria (Toomey & Ross 2011, cited). Framed as patterns, not diagnostic cutoffs. Kept.
3. **"Meals longer than 30 minutes, routinely, worth looking at."** Standard red flag in the pediatric feeding literature (Arvedson 2008; PFD materials). Kept.
4. **Repeated exposure claim.** Counter previously said "trials commonly use 10 to 15"; the classic literature is 8-15: Birch & Marlin (1982), Appetite 3(4):353-360 (2-year-olds needed 8-15 exposures); Wardle et al. (2003), cited, used 8 daily exposures and exposure beat reward. Counter now reads "research uses about 8 to 15," and Birch & Marlin added to sources. Headline "15+" kept as the conservative parent-facing number.
5. **Pressure backfires; rewards backfire.** Galloway et al. (2006), Appetite 46(3):318-323 ("Finish your soup": pressure reduced intake and worsened affect), now added to sources; Wardle 2003 showed the reward group did worse than exposure. Kept.
6. **Division of responsibility.** Satter (Ellyn Satter Institute), cited. Kept.
7. **Texture window in the second half of the first year.** Coulthard, Harris & Emmett (2009), Maternal & Child Nutrition 5(1):75-85 (ALSPAC: lumpy foods introduced after 9 months predicted more feeding problems and less variety at age 7), now added to sources. Kept.
8. **Developmental sequence** (readiness signs ~6 months; munching then lateralization 6-9; textures/cups 9-12; efficient chewing 12-18; rotary chew 2-3; appetite drop and neophobia peak in the twos). Consistent with Arvedson (2008, cited) and standard oral-motor development references; framed as a guide, not deadlines. Kept.
9. **Gagging vs choking distinction; forward gag reflex in infants.** Standard clinical content, consistent with ASHA portal. Kept.
10. **Safety signs list** (cough/choke, wet voice, color change, recurrent chest infections, etc.) and "clinical eval cannot see inside the throat; instrumental study when indicated": matches ASHA portal aspiration red flags and assessment guidance. Kept.
11. **ARFID mention** appropriately hedged ("a broader assessment... may be appropriate"), no diagnosis claims. Kept.
12. **Oral-motor exercises FAQ** correctly separates feeding-skill work (legitimate, food-based) from non-speech oral-motor exercises for speech (unsupported), consistent with the speech-sound page's McCauley 2009 review. Kept.
13. **Tube feeding FAQ** correctly defers weaning/volume decisions to physician and dietitian. Kept.
14. **Feeding Matters described as the Phoenix-based nonprofit that convened the PFD consensus.** Accurate, and a nice Arizona tie. Kept.

## Honest limits (kept visible on page or noted here)
- The steps-to-eating ladder and food chaining are widely used clinical frameworks (SOS: Toomey & Ross 2011; Fraker et al. 2007) with emerging rather than RCT-heavy evidence bases; the page presents them as method descriptions, not proven-outcome claims, which is the right register.
- The 30-food/20-food numbers are clinical rules of thumb, presented as "usually/around," not cutoffs.
- Exposure counts vary by study and age; page gives the range and keeps the counter conservative.
