# Understanding & Using Language page: accuracy review (August 2026)

Reviewed against the ASHA Practice Portal (Spoken Language Disorders), CATALISE, and primary research. Claim-by-claim verdicts; page updated same day. Same process as the AAC, autism, and speech-sound reviews.

## Verified and kept
1. **DLD prevalence ~7%, "two in an average classroom."** Tomblin et al. (1997), JSLHR 40(6):1245-1260: 7.4% of kindergartners (SLI). Norbury et al. (2016), JCPP 57(11):1247-1257 (SCALES population study): ~7.6% DLD. The two-per-classroom framing is RADLD's standard translation of the same figure. Both cited on page. Kept.
2. **DLD terminology and "not explained by another condition."** Matches CATALISE Phase 2 consensus (Bishop et al. 2017), cited. Kept.
3. **"No link to intelligence" counter and "full range of cognitive ability."** Consistent with CATALISE (nonverbal IQ criteria explicitly dropped) and Norbury 2016 (similar language profiles across NVIQ). Kept.
4. **"Not usually outgrown."** Consistent with longitudinal DLD literature and CATALISE persistence criteria; FAQ frames it practically. Kept.
5. **Grammar as a reliable marker.** Now backed in sources by Rice & Wexler (1996), JSHR 39(6):1239-1257 (tense marking as a clinical marker in English). Added.
6. **Emotional/behavioral association.** Yew & O'Kearney (2013) meta-analysis, cited. Kept.
7. **Reading claims (decoding vs comprehension, gap opens ~3rd-4th grade).** Simple View of Reading (Gough & Tunmer 1986) and Hulme & Snowling (2011), both cited; "poor comprehender" profile in the FAQ matches that literature. Kept.
8. **Bilingualism does not cause language disorders; disorder appears in all languages.** Consistent with Paradis, Genesee & Crago handbook (cited) and ASHA portal guidance. Kept.
9. **Recasting works.** Cleave et al. (2015), AJSLP 24(2):237-255, systematic review + meta-analysis: average effect sizes 0.96 (proximal) and 0.76 (distal); 18 of 20 grammar studies supported recasting. Page now states the near-1-SD result instead of a bare "it works"; added to sources.
10. **Narrative intervention as high-yield.** Petersen (2011) systematic review, cited. Kept.
11. **Home strategies and accommodations** (chunked instructions, wait time, comprehension checks, pre-taught vocabulary, graphic organizers) are standard clinical practice consistent with the ASHA portal; presented as advice, not statistics. Kept.

## Fixed
1. **Em dashes (3 instances)** violating the project rule, all in reference titles: CATALISE (before "terminology"), the "Spoken Language Disorders" portal title, and the RADLD entry. All replaced with colons.
2. **"Narrative skill predicts later reading and writing better than almost any other language measure"**: overclaimed. Narrative is a strong predictor, but no source supports "better than almost any other." Softened to "one of the stronger predictors of later reading and writing that can be measured in a young child."

## Added per Madison's voice (wit, kept honest)
- "If seven percent of children had a condition that affected their knees, everyone would know its name."
- "&quot;Do you understand?&quot; may be the only question in English with a fixed answer."
- Recasting result stated concretely (close to a full standard deviation) rather than vaguely.

## Honest limits (kept visible on page)
- "10 seconds of wait time" is a practice recommendation, consistent with processing-time guidance across the site, not a measured research dose.
- Prevalence figures come from population screening studies (Tomblin; Norbury); the page rounds to "around seven percent," which both support.
- Recast meta-analysis authors note evidence quality varied; page states the pooled result without promising it for every child.
