02

Services & fit

We work with children from infancy through the school-age years. Children can be assessed far earlier than most families expect: we use language measures normed from 8 months of age and speech measures from around 18 months, so there is rarely a reason to wait for a birthday before finding out where your child stands. We adapt goals and supports to each developmental stage, from early language development and school readiness to social connection and self-advocacy. If your child is older, reach out anyway and we will point you to a provider who fits.
Early communication and late talkers, autism and social communication, speech sound disorders, understanding and using language, stuttering and fluency, pediatric feeding and oral-motor skills, and AAC (augmentative and alternative communication). Every plan of care is personalized to what matters most to each client and their family. Reach out and we can talk through what would fit your child.
Your evaluation begins before we meet, with a review of your child’s history and what you’ve shared, so we know which questions the visit needs to answer. The appointment itself is play-based and in person, combining caregiver interview, observation, real play and conversation samples, and formal measures. The goal is to understand your whole child, not produce a single score. Depending on your concerns, we may look at speech sound production, understanding and using language, motor speech, fluency, social communication, feeding and oral-motor skills, or AAC. Afterward we score and analyze everything and write a comprehensive report in plain language covering what we found, what it means, and practical recommendations for home. A follow-up consultation to walk through it together is included in the evaluation fee, and therapy is not the automatic conclusion. If support isn’t needed, we’ll tell you.
We’ll meet with you to explain the results, answer questions, and review our recommendations. If therapy is recommended, we’ll create an individualized plan of care and discuss session length, frequency, scheduling, and priorities for home.
Sessions are personalized around your child’s goals, interests, and communication style. Depending on the child, therapy may include play, conversation, movement, structured practice, AAC, feeding activities, or coaching within everyday routines. We also share practical ideas for supporting progress between sessions.
Right now, every session is provided by a licensed speech-language pathologist. As our team grows, sessions may also be provided by a speech-language pathology assistant or a student clinician, always under the supervision of a certified SLP. You will know who your child is working with before the first session.
That’s okay. Children do not need to sit at a table or participate in a specific way for therapy to be valuable. We use movement, play, preferred activities, and your child’s interests to build connection and work toward meaningful goals.

Approach & philosophy

We believe different brains and communication styles belong. Our goal is not to make a child appear more typical. We do not target harmless stimming, scripting, or autistic traits simply because they look different. Instead, we support communication, autonomy, regulation, participation, and self-advocacy in ways that respect who your child already is.
No. Our goal is not to make your child look, sound, or act more typical. We help children communicate in ways that work for them, advocate for themselves, participate more fully in daily life, and feel understood, while respecting their individual identity and communication style.

Therapeutic play uses activities your child enjoys to work toward specific communication goals. Although it may look like ordinary play, the therapist carefully selects targets and materials, creates meaningful practice opportunities, and adjusts the level of support based on your child’s needs.

For example, if your child loves toy cars and is practicing a speech sound, the therapist may intentionally use words such as “car,” “key,” “crash,” or “cone.” Because the activity is engaging and connected to familiar play, your child is more likely to learn the skill and use it in everyday routines.

We believe communication begins with connection. Connection isn’t just the first step. It’s the foundation for confident, meaningful communication and lasting growth.

Families are never just another name on our schedule. We stay closely connected, celebrate progress, listen to recent challenges, and use what families share to guide each session.

Every visit begins with a check-in so we can meet your child where they are that day. Activities and support are adjusted based on their interests, energy, regulation, and needs. When a child feels hesitant or overwhelmed, connection comes first because children learn best when they feel safe, understood, and ready to engage.
